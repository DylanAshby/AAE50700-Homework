\section*{(5.1) Orbital Dynamics with $J_2$ and Atmospheric Drag}

\subsection*{(a): Given}

Consider a spacecraft of mass $m$ orbiting Earth perturbed by $J_2$ and atmosphere drag. Let the inertial frame be $\mathcal{N}:\{\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\}$ and write its Cartesian position as $\boldsymbol{r}=x\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x+y\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y+z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z,r=\|\boldsymbol{r}\|_2$. Expressing the potential energy for Earth gravity under $J_2$ in the Cartesian coordinates, we have
\begin{equation}
	V(x,y,z)=-\frac{\mu m}{r}+\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mu$ is Earth's gravitational parameter and $r_o=R_{\text{earth}}$ is its equatorial radius.\\

We use the same model as considered in PS1 to model the drag, which assumes an inertially stationary, spherically symmetric atmosphere, given by
\begin{equation}
	\boldsymbol{F}_D=m\boldsymbol{a}_D,\quad\boldsymbol{a}_D=-\frac{1}{2}C_D\frac{A}{m}\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}},\quad v\coloneqq\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_2,\quad\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}\coloneqq\frac{\boldsymbol{v}}{v},
	\label{eq:dragforce}
\end{equation}
where $\boldsymbol{v}=\dot{\boldsymbol{r}}$, $C_D$ is the drag coefficient, $A$ is the reference area, and
\begin{equation}
	\rho(r) \approx \rho_0 \exp\left[ -\frac{r - 6978~\mathrm{km}}{H} \right].
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{(a.1): Find}

From the Lagrangian using the Cartesian coordinates as generalized coordinates, $\boldsymbol{q}=[x,y,z]^T$.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

First recall that the kinetic energy $T$ of an object in motion can be expressed by half the mass times the square of the velocity:
\begin{equation}
	T(\boldsymbol{v})=\frac{m}{2}\boldsymbol{v}^T\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{mv^2}{2}
\end{equation}
\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	Then, recall the Lagrangian $L$ is the kinetic energy minus the potential energy $L=T-V$ ($V$ given by the problem definition):
	\begin{equation}
		L(\boldsymbol{r},\boldsymbol{v})=\frac{mv^2}{2}+\frac{\mu m}{r}-\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)
	\end{equation}
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(a.2): Find}

Derive the generalized force for Eq.~(\ref{eq:dragforce}) associated with $\boldsymbol{q}=[x,y,z]^T$.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

It is known that virtual work $\delta w$ is translational force $\boldsymbol{F}$ dotted with the Jacobian of position with respect to the generalized coordinates multiplied by the displacement of generalized coordinates. This also equals generalized force $\boldsymbol{Q}$ dotted with the same displacement:
\begin{equation}
	\delta w=\boldsymbol{F}^T\frac{\partial \boldsymbol{r}}{\partial \boldsymbol{q}}\delta\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{Q}^T\delta\boldsymbol{q}.
\end{equation}

Since in this case the generalized coordinates are the Cartesian position, $\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{r}$, the Jacobian is the identity matrix, and the generalized force is simply the applied force:
\begin{equation}
	\delta w = \boldsymbol{F}^T\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\delta\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{F}^T I\,\delta\boldsymbol{r}=\boldsymbol{F}^T\delta\boldsymbol{r}\quad\Rightarrow\quad\boldsymbol{Q}=\boldsymbol{F}.
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	So, from Eq.~(\ref{eq:dragforce}) there is only one force, which is the force of drag $\boldsymbol{F}_D$. This means the generalized force from that equation associated with $\boldsymbol{q}$ is $\boldsymbol{Q}=\boldsymbol{F}_D$. The mass terms cancel and simplify to get generalized force:
	\begin{equation}
		\boldsymbol{Q}=\boldsymbol{F}_D=m\boldsymbol{a}_D=m\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_D\frac{A}{m}\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}\right)=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}
	\end{equation}
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(a.3): Find}

Derive the Lagrange's equations and write the result as the Cartesian vector EOM
\begin{equation}
	\ddot{\boldsymbol{r}}=\boldsymbol{a}_{2B}+\boldsymbol{a}_{J2}+\boldsymbol{a}_D
	\label{eq:lagrange}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

We see here that this system has three degrees a freedom with no redundancy in coordinates, so $\boldsymbol{q}$ is a minimal coordinate set, and the system has no constraints on movement. Since there is also a non-conservative force (drag), the general form of Lagrange's equations with non-conservative general force may be used:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{q}}=\boldsymbol{Q}_{\text{nc}}
\end{equation}

And since, $\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{r}$ and $\boldsymbol{F}_D$ is the only non-conservative general force:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}=\boldsymbol{F}_{D}
	\label{eq:lagrangeorbitalcart}
\end{equation}

Then we compute the derivatives in Eq.~(\ref{eq:lagrangeorbitalcart}) individually.\\

So first to solve $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\frac{mv^2}{2}+\frac{\mu m}{r}-\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)\right) \\
		&=\frac{m}{2}\frac{\partial v^2}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\frac{\mu m}{r}\right)-\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\left(\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)\right) \\
		&=\frac{m}{2}2\boldsymbol{v}^T+0-0=m\boldsymbol{v}^T \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Then to solve $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\right)$:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{v}}\right)=m\frac{d\boldsymbol{v}^T}{dt}=m\boldsymbol{a}^T 
\end{equation}

Then solve $-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}&=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\left(\frac{mv^2}{2}+\frac{\mu m}{r}-\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)\right) \\
		&=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\left(\frac{mv^2}{2}\right)-\mu m\frac{\partial r^{-1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\left(\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)\right) \\
		&=0-\mu m\frac{\partial r^{-1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}+\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\left(\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)\right) \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Then for readability, we do the remaining component derivatives individually:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\partial r^{-1}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}=\frac{\partial r^{-1}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}=-r^{-2}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}^T
\end{equation}

And the other component:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\left(\frac{\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3z^2-r^2\right)\right)\\
		&=\frac{1}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\left(3r^{-5}z^2\right)-\frac{\partial r^{-3}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(3\left(\frac{\partial r^{-5}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}z^2+r^{-5}\frac{\partial z^2}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right)-\frac{\partial r^{-3}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(3\left(\frac{\partial r^{-5}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}z^2+r^{-5}\frac{\partial z^2}{\partial z}\frac{\partial z}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right)-\frac{\partial r^{-3}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(3\left(\frac{\partial r^{-5}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}z^2+r^{-5}\frac{\partial z^2}{\partial z}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right)-\frac{\partial r^{-3}}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(3\left((-5r^{-6})(\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}^T)z^2+2r^{-5}z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^TI\right)-(-3r^{-4}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}^T)\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(3\left((-5r^{-6})(r^{-1}\boldsymbol{r}^T)z^2+2r^{-5}z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^TI\right)-(-3r^{-4})(r^{-1}\boldsymbol{r}^T)\right) \\
		&=\frac{3}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2\left(-5r^{-7}z^2\boldsymbol{r}^T+2r^{-5}z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T+r^{-5}\boldsymbol{r}^T\right) \\
		&=\frac{3}{2}\mu mJ_2r_o^2r^{-5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}^T+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T\right) \\
		&=\frac{3\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}^T+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T\right)
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

So then that gives:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}&=-\mu m\left(-r^{-2}\hat{\boldsymbol{r}}^T\right)+\frac{3\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}^T+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T\right)\\
		&=\frac{\mu m}{r^3}\boldsymbol{r}^T+\frac{3\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}^T+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T\right)
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Which then plugging the above terms back into Eq.~(\ref{eq:lagrangeorbitalcart}) gives:
\begin{equation}
	m\boldsymbol{a}^T+\frac{\mu m}{r^3}\boldsymbol{r}^T+\frac{3\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}^T+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z^T\right)=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}^T
\end{equation}

Which then solved for acceleration $\boldsymbol{a}=\ddot{\boldsymbol{r}}$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		m\boldsymbol{a}&=-\frac{\mu m}{r^3}\boldsymbol{r}-\frac{3\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\right)-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}\\
		\boldsymbol{a}&=-\frac{\mu m}{r^3 m}\boldsymbol{r}-\frac{3\mu mJ_2r_o^2}{2r^5 m}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\right)-\frac{1}{2m}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}} \\
		\boldsymbol{a}&=-\frac{\mu}{r^3}\boldsymbol{r}-\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\right)-\frac{1}{2m}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}} \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	Which means the Cartesian vector EOM are:
	\begin{equation}
		\ddot{\boldsymbol{r}}=-\frac{\mu}{r^3}\boldsymbol{r}-\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\right)-\frac{1}{2m}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}}
	\end{equation}
	Where:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			\boldsymbol{a}_{2B}&=-\frac{\mu}{r^3}\boldsymbol{r} \\
			\boldsymbol{a}_{J2}&=-\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(\left(1-\frac{5z^2}{r^2}\right)\boldsymbol{r}+2z\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\right) \\
			\boldsymbol{a}_D&=-\frac{1}{2m}C_DA\rho(r)v^2\hat{\boldsymbol{v}} \\
		\end{split}
	\end{equation}
	Since these terms come from the two-body problem, $J_2$ perturbation, and drag acceleration components of the system respectively.
\end{mdframed}

\subsection*{(b): Given}

Now consider spherical coordinates $\boldsymbol{q}=[r,\phi,\theta]^T$, where $r$ is radius, $\phi$ is azimuth about $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$, and $\theta$ is elevation measured from $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x-\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y$ plane. The potential energy for gravity with $J_2$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
	V=-\frac{m\mu}{r}\left[1-J_2\left(\frac{r_o}{r}\right)^2P_2(\sin\theta)\right]
	\label{eq:gravpotential}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection*{(b.1): Find}

Form the Lagrangian using spherical coordinates.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

The Lagrangian is once again found by kinetic minus potential energy $L=T-V$. The gravity potential including $J_2$ zonal harmonics was already given by Eq.~(\ref{eq:gravpotential}), then the kinetic energy is once again given by:
\begin{equation}
	T=\frac{mv^2}{2}
	\label{eq:kinetic}
\end{equation}

To express $T$ in spherical coordinates, we write the position as $\boldsymbol{r}=r\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$ in the spherical basis $\mathcal{S}:\{\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\}$. Since this basis rotates relative to the inertial basis $\mathcal{N}:\{\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\}$, we find $\boldsymbol{v}$ with the transport theorem, which requires the rotation of the spherical basis with respect to the inertial Cartesian basis, $\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{N}}$. We can represent this by considering $\dot{\phi}$ scaled by the $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$ components of $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z=\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$, as well as $\dot{\theta}$ scaled by the negative direction of $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi$:
\begin{equation}
	\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{N}}=\dot{\phi}\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+\dot{\phi}\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta-\dot{\theta}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi
\end{equation}

Which we then find $\boldsymbol{v}$ via the transport theorem as:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\boldsymbol{v}&=\frac{\prescript{\mathcal{S}}{}{d}}{dt}\boldsymbol{r}+\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{N}}\times\boldsymbol{r} \\
		&=\frac{\prescript{\mathcal{S}}{}{d}}{dt}\left(r\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\right)+\boldsymbol{\omega}_{\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{N}}\times\left(r\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\right) \\
		&=\frac{\prescript{\mathcal{S}}{}{d}r}{dt}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+r\frac{\prescript{\mathcal{S}}{}{d}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r}{dt}+r\left(\dot{\phi}\sin\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+\dot{\phi}\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta-\dot{\theta}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\right)\times\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r \\
		&=\dot{r}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+r(0)+r\left(\dot{\phi}\sin\theta\,(\boldsymbol{0})+\dot{\phi}\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi-\dot{\theta}\left(-\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\right)\right) \\
		&=\dot{r}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+r\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi+r\dot{\theta}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Then we can simply use the $\mathcal{S}$-frame representation of $\boldsymbol{v}$ to find an expression for the kinetic energy $T$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		T&=\frac{mv^2}{2}=\frac{m}{2}\boldsymbol{v}^T\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{m}{2}(v_r^2+v_\phi^2+v_\theta^2) \\
		&=\frac{m}{2}\left(\dot{r}^2+(r\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi})^2+(r\dot{\theta})^2\right) \\
		&=\frac{m}{2}\left(\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right) \\
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:kineticeval}
\end{equation}

So, the Lagrangian can be written as:
\begin{equation}
	L=\frac{m}{2}\left(\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right)+\frac{m\mu}{r}\left[1-J_2\left(\frac{r_o}{r}\right)^2P_2(\sin\theta)\right]
\end{equation}

However, this expression still needs to have substituted in the second degree, zeroth order, Legendre polynomial $P_2(\sin\theta)$, which can be found by the following formula:
\begin{equation}
	P_n(x)=\sum_{m=0}^{M}(-1)^m\frac{(2n-2m)!}{2^n m!(n-m)!(n-2m)!}x^{n-2m}
\end{equation} 
Where $n$ is degree and $M=n/2$.\\

Evaluating it becomes:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		P_2(\sin\theta)&=\sum_{m=0}^{1}(-1)^m\frac{(2(2)-2m)!}{2^2\,m!\,(2-m)!\,(2-2m)!}\sin^{2-2m}\theta \\
		&=(-1)^0\frac{(4-0)!}{2^2\,(0)!\,(2-0)!\,(2-0)!}\sin^{2}\theta+(-1)^1\frac{(4-2)!}{2^2\,(1)!\,(2-1)!\,(2-2)!}\sin^{0}\theta \\
		&=\frac{24}{4\cdot1\cdot2\cdot2}\sin^2\theta-\frac{2}{4\cdot1\cdot1\cdot1} \\
		&=\frac{3}{2}\sin^2\theta-\frac{1}{2}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	So then substituting into the found Lagrangian, we get its proper form:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			L&=\frac{m}{2}\left(\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right)+\frac{m\mu}{r}\left(1-J_2\left(\frac{r_o}{r}\right)^2\left(\frac{3}{2}\sin^2\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right) \\
			&=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^3}\left(\frac{3}{2}\sin^2\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right) \\
			&=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}
			\label{eq:lagrangianb}
		\end{split}
	\end{equation}
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(b.2): Find}

Derive the generalized force for Eq.~(\ref{eq:dragforce}) associated with $\boldsymbol{q}=[r,\phi,\theta]^T$.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

In part (a.2), $\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{r}$ made the Jacobian in $\boldsymbol{Q}=\boldsymbol{F}_D^T\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\boldsymbol{q}}$ the identity matrix, but now that $\boldsymbol{q}=[r,\phi,\theta]^T$, we must find how the position changes with respect to $r$, $\phi$, and $\theta$.\\

Let us consider the total derivative of $\boldsymbol{r}$ from the perspective of the chain rule:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d\boldsymbol{r}}{dt}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial r}\dot{r}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\phi}\dot{\phi}+\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\theta}\dot{\theta}=\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\boldsymbol{q}}\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}
\end{equation}

Then put it side by side with the expression found for $\boldsymbol{v}=\frac{d\boldsymbol{r}}{dt}$:
\begin{equation}
	\boldsymbol{v}=\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\dot{r}+r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\dot{\phi}+r\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\dot{\theta}
\end{equation}

Thus from this we see that:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial r}=\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r,\quad\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\phi}=r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi,\quad\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\theta}=r\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta
\end{equation}

So when we compute the generalized force using $Q_j=\boldsymbol{F}_D\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial q_j}$
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		Q_r&=\boldsymbol{F}_D\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial r}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\boldsymbol{v}\right)\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\dot{r}+r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\dot{\phi}+r\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\dot{\theta}\right)\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,\dot{r} \\
		\\
		Q_\phi&=\boldsymbol{F}_D\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\phi}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\boldsymbol{v}\right)\cdot\left(r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\right) \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\dot{r}+r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\dot{\phi}+r\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\dot{\theta}\right)\cdot\left(r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\right) \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi} \\
		\\
		Q_\theta&=\boldsymbol{F}_D\cdot\frac{\partial\boldsymbol{r}}{\partial\theta}=\left(-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\boldsymbol{v}\right)\cdot\left(r\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\right) \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\left(\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\dot{r}+r\cos\theta\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi\dot{\phi}+r\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\dot{\theta}\right)\cdot\left(r\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta\right) \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	Which makes the generalized force:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			Q_r&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,\dot{r} \\
			Q_\phi&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi} \\
			Q_\theta&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta}
		\end{split}
		\label{eq:forcenc}
	\end{equation}
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(b.3): Find}

Derive the Lagrange's equations and write the result as $\ddot{r}=?$, $\ddot{\phi}=?$, and $\ddot{\theta}=?$.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

Once again this system has a minimal coordinate set and a non-conservative force from drag. So, the general form of Lagrange's equations with non-conservative general force may be used:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\boldsymbol{q}}=\boldsymbol{Q}_{\text{nc}}
	\label{eq:lagrangenc}
\end{equation}

First, we evaluate the generalized momentum terms:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\left(\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\right) \\
		&=0+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta}{2}\frac{\partial\dot{\phi}^2}{\partial\dot{\phi}}+0+0-0+0=mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta \\
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:phimomentum}
\end{equation}

And the $\theta$ momentum:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\theta}}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{\theta}}\left(\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\right) \\
		&=0+0+\frac{mr^2}{2}\frac{\partial\dot{\theta}^2}{\partial\dot{\theta}}+0-0+0=mr^2\dot{\theta} \\
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:thetamomentum}
\end{equation}

And the $r$ momentum:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{r}}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\dot{r}}\left(\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\right) \\
		&=\frac{m}{2}\frac{\partial\dot{r}^2}{\partial\dot{r}}+0+0+0-0+0=m\dot{r}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:rmomentum}
\end{equation}

Then we compute their time derivatives:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)&=\frac{d}{dt}(mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta) \\
		&=m\left(\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta\frac{dr^2}{dt}+r^2\cos^2\theta\frac{d\dot{\phi}}{dt}+r^2\dot{\phi}\frac{d}{dt}\cos^2\theta\right) \\
		&=m\left(\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta\frac{dr^2}{dr}\frac{dr}{dt}+r^2\cos^2\theta\frac{d\dot{\phi}}{dt}+r^2\dot{\phi}\frac{d}{d\theta}(\cos^2\theta)\frac{d\theta}{dt}\right) \\
		&=m\left(2r\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta+r^2\ddot{\phi}\cos^2\theta-2r^2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\cos\theta\sin\theta\right) \\
		&=mr\cos\theta\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta+r\ddot{\phi}\cos\theta-2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\right)
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:phimomentumdt}
\end{equation}

And the $\theta$ time derivative:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\theta}}\right)=\frac{d}{dt}(mr^2\dot{\theta})=m\left(\frac{dr^2}{dr}\frac{dr}{dt}\dot{\theta}+r^2\frac{d\dot{\theta}}{dt}\right)=2mr\dot{r}\dot{\theta}+mr^2\ddot{\theta}=mr\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}+r\ddot{\theta}\right)
\end{equation}

And the $r$ time derivative:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{r}}\right)=m\frac{d\dot{r}}{dt}=m\ddot{r}
\end{equation}

Since the Lagrangian is not a function of $\phi$:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi}=0
\end{equation}

Then compute the $\theta$ derivative:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta}&=\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\right) \\
		&=0+\frac{mr^2\dot{\phi}^2}{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\cos^2\theta\right)+0+0-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\left(\sin^2\theta\right)+0 \\
		&=-mr^2\dot{\phi}^2\cos\theta\sin\theta-3m\mu J_2r_o^2r^{-3}\cos\theta\sin\theta \\
		&=-m\cos\theta\sin\theta\left(r^2\dot{\phi}^2+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^3}\right) \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

And the $r$ derivative:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&=\frac{\partial L}{\partial r} \\
		&=\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\left(\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\right) \\
		&=0+\frac{m\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}\frac{dr^2}{dr}+\frac{m\dot{\theta}^2}{2}\frac{dr^2}{dr}+m\mu\frac{dr^{-1}}{dr}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2}\sin^2\theta\frac{dr^{-3}}{dr}+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2}\frac{dr^{-3}}{dr} \\
		&=mr\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+mr\dot{\theta}^2-m\mu r^{-2}+\frac{9}{2}m\mu J_2r_o^2r^{-4}\sin^2\theta-\frac{3}{2}m\mu J_2r_o^2r^{-4} \\
		&=mr\left(\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+\dot{\theta}^2-\mu r^{-3}+\frac{9}{2}\mu J_2r_o^2r^{-5}\sin^2\theta-\frac{3}{2}\mu J_2r_o^2r^{-5}\right) \\
		&=mr\left(\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^3}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right)\right) \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

So, as per Eq.~(\ref{eq:lagrangenc}), we can then equate the above calculated terms to their respective non-conservative generalized drag force to get the equations of motion.\\

For the coordinate $\phi$ we set up the EoM as:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi}&=Q_{\phi,\text{nc}} \\
		mr\cos\theta\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta+r\ddot{\phi}\cos\theta-2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\right)-0&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)vr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi} \\	
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

And we then isolate the $\ddot{\phi}$ term:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\ddot{\phi}\,mr^2\cos^2\theta&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}\,r^2\cos^2\theta-2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\,mr\cos^2\theta+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\,mr^2\cos\theta \\
		\ddot{\phi}&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}\frac{r^2\cos^2\theta}{mr^2\cos^2\theta}-2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\frac{mr\cos^2\theta}{mr^2\cos^2\theta}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}\frac{mr^2\cos\theta}{mr^2\cos\theta} \\
		\ddot{\phi}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}}{r}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\tan\theta
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

For the coordinate $\theta$ we set up the EoM as:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\theta}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\theta}&=Q_{\theta,\text{nc}} \\
		mr\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}+r\ddot{\theta}\right)+m\cos\theta\sin\theta\left(r^2\dot{\phi}^2+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^3}\right)&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta} \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

And we then isolate the $\ddot{\theta}$ term:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\ddot{\theta}\,mr^2&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}\,r^2-2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}\,mr-\dot{\phi}^2\cos\theta\sin\theta\,mr^2-3\mu J_2r_o^2\cos\theta\sin\theta\,\frac{m}{r^3} \\
		\ddot{\theta}&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}\frac{r^2}{mr^2}-2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}\frac{mr}{mr^2}-\dot{\phi}^2\cos\theta\sin\theta\frac{mr^2}{mr^2}-3\mu J_2r_o^2\cos\theta\sin\theta\frac{m}{r^3}\frac{1}{mr^2} \\
		\ddot{\theta}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r}-\dot{\phi}^2\cos\theta\sin\theta-\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^5}\cos\theta\sin\theta \\
		\ddot{\theta}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r}-\cos\theta\sin\theta\left(\dot{\phi}^2+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^5}\right)
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

For the coordinate $r$ we set up the EoM as:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{r}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial r}&=Q_{r,\text{nc}} \\
		m\ddot{r}-mr\left(\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^3}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right)\right)&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r} \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

And we then isolate the $\ddot{r}$ term:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\ddot{r}m&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r}+r\left(\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^3}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right)\right)m \\
		\ddot{r}&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r}\frac{1}{m}+r\left(\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^3}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right)\right)\frac{m}{m} \\
		\ddot{r}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r}}{2m}+r\left(\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^3}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^5}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right)\right) \\
		\ddot{r}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r}}{2m}+r\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+r\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^2}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^4}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right)
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	Which makes the EoM:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			\ddot{r}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r}}{2m}+r\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+r\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^2}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^4}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right) \\
			\ddot{\theta}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r}-\cos\theta\sin\theta\left(\dot{\phi}^2+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^5}\right) \\
			\ddot{\phi}&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}}{r}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\tan\theta \\
			&\text{Where:  }v=\sqrt{\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2}
		\end{split}
		\label{eq:eom}
	\end{equation}
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(b.4): Find}

For zero drag, identify the cyclic coordinate and the associated conserved generalized momentum. Discuss its relation to the angular momentum about $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$, denoted by $H_z$.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	It was identified in (b.3) that the Lagrangian is not a function of $\phi$. \textbf{This means that $\phi$ is the cyclic coordinate}.\\
	
	If the force of drag is removed, this removes the only non-conservative force, and the Lagrange equation for $\phi$ now evaluates as:
	\begin{equation}
		\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)-\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi}=0\Rightarrow\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)=0
	\end{equation}
	Because $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi}=0$ as determined in (b.3), the generalized momentum $p_\phi$ is constant over time.\\
	
	Likewise, the equation for this constant generalized momentum was also found in (b.3) Eq.~(\ref{eq:phimomentum}):
	\begin{equation}
		p_{\phi}=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}=mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta
	\end{equation}
\end{mdframed}

To compare $p_\phi$ to $H_z$, we take the $z$ component of $\boldsymbol{r}\times\boldsymbol{p}$, using the same relation between the spherical and Cartesian bases used to derive $\boldsymbol{v}$:
\begin{equation}
	\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z=\sin\theta\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+\cos\theta\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	So using this, with the previously found values (b.1) for $\boldsymbol{r}$ and $\boldsymbol{v}$ we get:
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			H_z&=\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\cdot(\boldsymbol{r}\times\boldsymbol{p})=\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\cdot(\boldsymbol{r}\times m\boldsymbol{v}) \\
			&=(\sin\theta\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+\cos\theta\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta)\cdot(r\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\times(m\dot{r}\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+mr\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi+mr\dot{\theta}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta)) \\
			&=(\sin\theta\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+\cos\theta\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta)\cdot(mr\dot{r}(\boldsymbol{0})+mr^2\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta+mr^2\dot{\theta}(-\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi)) \\
			&=\sin\theta(0)+\cos\theta\left(mr^2\cos\theta\,\dot{\phi}\right)+0 \\
			&=mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta=p_\phi
		\end{split}
		\label{eq:zmomentum}
	\end{equation}
	So from this derivation we see that \textbf{$p_\phi$ is equivalent to the angular momentum about the $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$ axis $H_z$}.
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(b.5): Find}

For zero drag, show that the Jacobi energy integral
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{J}\coloneqq\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}^T\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}}-L
\end{equation}
is identical to the total energy $E=T+V$. Discuss if $\mathcal{J}$ is conserved under no drag, and why.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

The total energy $E$ computes just like the Lagrangian in Eq.~(\ref{eq:lagrangianb}), but the potential is added instead of subtracted. Likewise, the expression ends up looking quite similar:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		E&=T+V \\
		&=\frac{m}{2}\left(\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right)-\frac{m\mu}{r}\left(1-J_2\left(\frac{r_o}{r}\right)^2\left(\frac{3}{2}\sin^2\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right)\right) \\
		&=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}-\frac{m\mu}{r}+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^3}\left(\frac{3}{2}\sin^2\theta-\frac{1}{2}\right) \\
		&=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}-\frac{m\mu}{r}+\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta-\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Then to find the Jacobi integral, first we compute $\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}^T\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}}$. This is a dot product between the coordinate rates and the generalized momentum found in (b.3) in equations (\ref{eq:phimomentum}), (\ref{eq:thetamomentum}), and (\ref{eq:rmomentum}):
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}^T\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}}&=\dot{r}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{r}}+\dot{\theta}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\theta}}+\dot{\phi}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}} \\
		&=\dot{r}\left(m\dot{r}\right)+\dot{\theta}\left(mr^2\dot{\theta}\right)+\dot{\phi}\left(mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta\right) \\
		&=m\dot{r}^2+mr^2\dot{\theta}^2+mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

\newpage

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	Then simply subtract the Lagrangian. We see below that this ends up flipping all the signs that were different between the Lagrangian and the total energy, and this ends up showing that the Jacobi integral is equal to the total energy:
	\small
	\begin{equation}
		\begin{split}
			\mathcal{J}&=\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}^T\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}}-L \\
			&=\left(m\dot{r}^2+mr^2\dot{\theta}^2+mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2\right) \\
			&\quad-\left(\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\right) \\
			&=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}-\frac{m\mu}{r}+\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta-\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}=E
		\end{split}
		\label{eq:jacobiintegral}
	\end{equation}
	\normalsize
	In our zero drag system, \textbf{the Jacobi integral is conserved}. This is because the Jacobi integral represents generalized total energy, and for it to be conserved it must not change over time. It will change over time if: there are non-conservative forces breaking conservation of energy in the system, or there is time variance in the Lagrangian, since $\mathcal{J}$ is purely a function of $L$. There are no non-conservative forces, and we see from (b.3) Eq.~(\ref{eq:lagrangianb}) that $L$ has no explicit dependence on time so $\mathcal{J}$ is conserved.
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(b.6): Find}

Under drag, derive the time derivative of $H_z$ and $\mathcal{J}$. Discuss if they are conserved and why.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

As discussed in (b.4), $H_z=p_\phi=\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}$, thus the time derivative $\frac{d}{dt}(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}})$ found in Eq.~(\ref{eq:phimomentumdt}) is equivalent to the time derivative of $H_z$:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d}{dt}H_z=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)=mr\cos\theta\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta+r\ddot{\phi}\cos\theta-2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\right)
\end{equation}

Then, when substituting the derived EoM for $\ddot{\phi}$ from Eq.~(\ref{eq:eom}) into the time derivative of $H_z$, we see the equation reduce to:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{dH_z}{dt}&=mr\cos\theta\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta+r\ddot{\phi}\cos\theta-2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\right) \\
		&=mr\cos\theta\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta+r\cos\theta\left(-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}}{r}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\tan\theta\right)-2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\right) \\
		&=mr\cos\theta\left(2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)vr\dot{\phi}\cos\theta}{2m}-2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}\cos\theta+2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta-2r\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\sin\theta\right) \\
		&=mr\cos\theta\left(-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)vr\dot{\phi}\cos\theta}{2m}\right) \\
		&=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v}{2m}\left(mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta\right)=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v}{2m}H_z
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

The time rate of the Jacobi integral $\mathcal{J}$ is given by:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d\mathcal{J}}{dt}=\boldsymbol{Q}_{\text{nc}}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}
	\label{eq:jacobirate}
\end{equation}

The dot product portion of Eq.~(\ref{eq:jacobirate}) is between non-conservative forces found in Eq.~(\ref{eq:forcenc}) and the generalized coordinate rate, and it evaluates to:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\boldsymbol{Q}_{\text{nc}}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}&=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			Q_r & Q_\phi & Q_\theta \\
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\dot{r} \\
			\dot{\phi} \\
			\dot{\theta} \\
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		&=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,\dot{r} & -\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi} & -\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta} \\
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\dot{r} \\
			\dot{\phi} \\
			\dot{\theta} \\
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,\dot{r}^2-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta}^2 \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\left(\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right) \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^3
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
The portion in the parenthesis ends up being equivalent to $\|\boldsymbol{v}\|_2^2$, so it simplifies to a fairly compact scalar function.\\

Looking at the Lagrangian $L$:
\begin{equation}
	L=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}+\frac{m\mu}{r}-\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta+\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}
\end{equation}
As discussed in (b.5), there are no explicit time $t$ terms, so the partial $\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}=0$.\\

Which makes the time derivative of $\mathcal{J}$ equal to:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{d\mathcal{J}}{dt}=\boldsymbol{Q}_{\text{nc}}\cdot\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}-\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^3-0=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^3
\end{equation}

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	So, to reiterate, the time derivatives of $H_z$ and $\mathcal{J}$ are:
	\begin{equation}
		\frac{d}{dt}H_z=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v}{2m}H_z,\quad\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^3
	\end{equation}
	
	We see that \textbf{$H_z$ and $\mathcal{J}$ are not conserved} because their rates of change are continuously negative scaling with air density and speed. Since $\rho(r)>0$ and $v\geq0$, the sign never flips, and both $\mathcal{J}$ and the magnitude of $H_z$ always decrease under drag.
\end{mdframed}

\textbf{Define Reference Frames}:
\begin{itemize}
	\item \textbf{$\mathcal{N}$:\{$\mathcal{O}$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$\}: Earth-centered inertial reference frame (ECI)\\}An Earth-centered inertial reference frame with its origin at Earth center having $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$ aligned with the Earth's rotational axis pointing towards the north pole.
	\item \textbf{$\mathcal{S}$:\{$\mathcal{O}$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$\}: Spherical frame\\}A frame with its origin at Earth center where $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$ is aligned with the spacecraft's current position vector, $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\phi$ points in the direction of increasing azimuth $\phi$ about $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$ (measured from $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x$), and $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_\theta$ points in the direction of increasing elevation $\theta$ (measured from the equatorial $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x$-$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y$ plane toward the north pole).
	\item \textbf{$\mathcal{B}$:\{$\mathcal{O}$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_u$,$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_h$\}: Radial Transverse Normal frame (RTN)\\}A body frame with its origin at Earth center where $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$ is aligned with the spacecraft's current position vector, $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_u$ is aligned with the spacecraft's local horizon in the direction of increasing argument of latitude $u=\omega+\nu$, and $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_h$ is aligned with the orbit's specific angular momentum vector.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection*{(c.1): Find}

Express the initial conditions in the Cartesian state $(x,y,z,\dot{x},\dot{y},\dot{z})$ and spherical state $(r,\phi,\theta,\dot{r},\dot{\phi},\dot{\theta})$.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

Converting from Keplerian orbital elements to Cartesian states in this context is fairly simple. The initial true anomaly is given as $\nu_0=0$, so the magnitudes of spacecraft position $\boldsymbol{r}$ and velocity $\boldsymbol{v}$ can be calculated directly using the below equations, which include semi-major axis $a$, semi-latus rectum $p$, eccentricity $e$, and gravitational parameter $\mu$.
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		r=\frac{p}{1+e\cos{\nu}},\quad p=a(1-e^2),\quad v=\sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r}-\frac{\mu}{a}} \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Which evaluate to:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		p&=(6628\text{ km})(1-0.01^2)=6627.3372\text{ km} \\
		r&=\frac{6627.3372\text{ km}}{1+0.01\cos(0\text{ rad})}=6561.72\text{ km} \\
		v&=\sqrt{\frac{2(398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2)}{6561.72\text{ km}}-\frac{398600\text{ km}^3\text{/s}^2}{6628\text{ km}}}\approx7.8329\text{ km/s}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

Since $\nu_0=0$, the spacecraft starts at periapsis, where the velocity is perpendicular to the position vector. So in the RTN frame, $\boldsymbol{r}$ lies entirely along $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r$ and $\boldsymbol{v}$ lies entirely along $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_u$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\boldsymbol{r}&=r\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+0\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_u+0\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_h=6561.72\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r\text{ km} \\
		\boldsymbol{v}&=0\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_r+v\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_u+0\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_h\approx7.8329\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_u\text{ km/s}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:rtnrv}
\end{equation}

Then the RTN frame $\boldsymbol{r}$ and $\boldsymbol{v}$ vectors can be converted to ECI frame using the DCM described below, which includes right ascension of the ascending node $\Omega$, inclination $i$, and argument of latitude $u=\omega+\nu$. This DCM represents a 3-1-3 euler sequence that converts RTN to ECI coordinates.
\begin{equation}
	\left[
	\begin{matrix}
		\cos{\Omega}\cos{u}-\sin{\Omega}\cos{i}\sin{u} & -\cos{\Omega}\sin{u}-\sin{\Omega}\cos{i}\cos{u} & \sin{\Omega}\sin{i}\\
		\sin{\Omega}\cos{u}+\cos{\Omega}\cos{i}\sin{u} & -\sin{\Omega}\sin{u}+\cos{\Omega}\cos{i}\cos{u} & -\cos{\Omega}\sin{i}\\
		\sin{i}\sin{u} & \sin{i}\cos{u} & \cos{i}
	\end{matrix}
	\right]
\end{equation}

We see from table (\ref*{tab:reference_orbit}) that the initial right ascension $\Omega_0$, argument of periapsis $\omega_0$, and true anomaly $\nu_0$ are all zero, and the initial inclination $i_0$ is $45$ degrees. This makes the DCM evaluate to:
\begin{equation}
	\footnotesize
	\begin{split}
		[NB]&=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\cos{0}\cos{0}-\sin{0}\cos{45^\circ}\sin{0} & -\cos{0}\sin{0}-\sin{0}\cos{45^\circ}\cos{0} & \sin{0}\sin{45^\circ}\\
			\sin{0}\cos{0}+\cos{0}\cos{45^\circ}\sin{0} & -\sin{0}\sin{0}+\cos{0}\cos{45^\circ}\cos{0} & -\cos{0}\sin{45^\circ}\\
			\sin{45^\circ}\sin{0} & \sin{45^\circ}\cos{0} & \cos{45^\circ}
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		&=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			1 & 0 & 0 \\
			0 & \cos 45^\circ & -\sin 45^\circ \\
			0 & \sin 45^\circ & \cos 45^\circ
		\end{bmatrix}
		=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			1 & 0 & 0 \\
			0 & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
			0 & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
		\end{bmatrix}
	\end{split}
	\normalsize
\end{equation}
Which is effectively just a 45 degree rotation about the $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x$ axis.\\

Likewise we see this rotation applied to the RTN frame values of $\boldsymbol{r}$ and $\boldsymbol{v}$ in Eq.~(\ref{eq:rtnrv}):
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\prescript{\mathcal{N}}{}{\boldsymbol{r}}&=[NB]\,\prescript{\mathcal{B}}{}{\boldsymbol{r}}=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			1 & 0 & 0 \\
			0 & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
			0 & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			6561.72 \\
			0 \\
			0
		\end{bmatrix}\text{ km}
		=6561.72\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x\text{ km} \\
		\prescript{\mathcal{N}}{}{\boldsymbol{v}}&=[NB]\,\prescript{\mathcal{B}}{}{\boldsymbol{v}}\approx
		\begin{bmatrix}
			1 & 0 & 0 \\
			0 & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & -\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} \\
			0 & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2} & \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			0 \\
			7.8329 \\
			0
		\end{bmatrix}\text{ km/s}
		\approx5.5387\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y+5.5387\,\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z\text{ km/s}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
which gives the Cartesian state initial conditions.\\

Then to convert to spherical coordinates, we use the following relationships between Cartesian and spherical coordinates, where $r$ is the norm of $\boldsymbol{r}$ and $\rho$ is the length of the projection of $\boldsymbol{r}$ onto the $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_x$-$\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_y$ plane:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\rho&=r\cos\theta,\quad x=\rho\cos\phi=r\cos\theta\cos\phi,\\
		z&=r\sin\theta,\quad y=\rho\sin\phi=r\cos\theta\sin\phi,\\
		r&=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}=\sqrt{\rho^2+z^2} \\
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:cartsphere}
\end{equation}

In Eq.~(\ref{eq:cartsphere}), the conversion for the $r$ coordinate is already found (which is just the norm of $\boldsymbol{r}$). Then we can derive expressions for $\phi$ and $\theta$ by dividing $y$ by $x$ and $z$ by $\rho$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{y}{x}&=\frac{\rho\sin\phi}{\rho\cos\phi}=\frac{\sin\phi}{\cos\phi}=\tan\phi\quad\Rightarrow\quad\phi=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right) \\
		\frac{z}{\rho}&=\frac{r\sin\theta}{r\cos\theta}=\frac{\sin\theta}{\cos\theta}=\tan\theta\quad\Rightarrow\quad\theta=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{z}{\rho}\right)=\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{z}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}\right) \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
And by utilizing the atan2 algorithm in Matlab, these derived expressions can be used to compute these angles while resolving the quadrant ambiguity.\\

Finding the derivative components $\dot{r}$, $\dot{\phi}$, and $\dot{\theta}$ then requires a derivative of the expressions used to convert from Cartesian to spherical:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&\text{Let: }u=x^2+y^2+z^2 \\
		\\
		\dot{r}&=\frac{d}{dt}r=\frac{d}{du}\left(u^{1/2}\right)\frac{du}{dt} \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}u^{-1/2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(x^2+y^2+z^2\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}u^{-1/2}\left(\frac{d(x^2)}{dx}\frac{dx}{dt}+\frac{d(y^2)}{dy}\frac{dy}{dt}+\frac{d(z^2)}{dz}\frac{dz}{dt}\right) \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}u^{-1/2}\left(2x\dot{x}+2y\dot{y}+2z\dot{z}\right) \\
		&=\frac{x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}+z\dot{z}}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}}=\frac{x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}+z\dot{z}}{r}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

And the derivative to get $\dot{\phi}$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&\text{Let: }u=\frac{y}{x} \\
		\\
		\dot{\phi}&=\frac{d}{dt}\phi=\frac{d}{du}\left(\tan^{-1}u\right)\frac{du}{dt}=\frac{1}{1+u^2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)=\frac{1}{1+u^2}\left(\frac{x\frac{dy}{dt}-y\frac{dx}{dt}}{x^2}\right)= \\
		&=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{y}{x}\right)^2}\left(\frac{x\dot{y}-y\dot{x}}{x^2}\right)=\frac{x^2}{x^2+y^2}\left(\frac{x\dot{y}-y\dot{x}}{x^2}\right)=\frac{x\dot{y}-y\dot{x}}{x^2+y^2}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

And the derivative to get $\dot{\theta}$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		&\text{Let: }u=\frac{z}{\rho},\quad\rho=\sqrt{x^2+y^2},\quad w=x^2+y^2 \\
		\\
		\dot{\rho}&=\frac{d}{dt}\rho=\frac{d}{dw}\left(w^{1/2}\right)\frac{dw}{dt}=\frac{1}{2}w^{-1/2}\left(\frac{d(x^2)}{dx}\frac{dx}{dt}+\frac{d(y^2)}{dy}\frac{dy}{dt}\right)= \\
		&=\frac{1}{2}w^{-1/2}\left(2x\dot{x}+2y\dot{y}\right)=\frac{x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}}{\sqrt{x^2+y^2}}=\frac{x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}}{\rho} \\
		\\
		\dot{\theta}&=\frac{d}{dt}\theta=\frac{d}{du}\left(\tan^{-1}u\right)\frac{du}{dt}=\frac{1}{1+u^2}\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{z}{\rho}\right)=\frac{1}{1+u^2}\left(\frac{\rho\frac{dz}{dt}-z\frac{d\rho}{dt}}{\rho^2}\right)= \\
		&=\frac{1}{1+\left(\frac{z}{\rho}\right)^2}\left(\frac{\rho\dot{z}-z\dot{\rho}}{\rho^2}\right)=\frac{\rho^2}{\rho^2+z^2}\left(\frac{\rho\dot{z}-z\dot{\rho}}{\rho^2}\right)=\frac{\rho\dot{z}-z\dot{\rho}}{\rho^2+z^2}=\frac{\rho\dot{z}-z\dot{\rho}}{r^2}= \\
				&=\frac{\rho\dot{z}-z\left(\frac{x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}}{\rho}\right)}{r^2}=\frac{\rho^2\dot{z}-z\left(x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}\right)}{\rho r^2} \\
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

So, we then find spherical states $r$, $\phi$, $\theta$, $\dot{r}$, $\dot{\phi}$, and $\dot{\theta}$:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		r&=\sqrt{x^2+y^2+z^2}=\sqrt{(6561.72)^2+(0)^2+(0)^2}=6561.72\text{ km} \\
		\phi&=\operatorname{atan2}(y,x)=\operatorname{atan2}(0,6561.72)=0^\circ \\
		\theta&=\operatorname{atan2}\left(z,\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\right)=\operatorname{atan2}\left(0,\sqrt{(6561.72)^2+(0)^2}\right)=0^\circ \\
		\dot{r}&=\frac{x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}+z\dot{z}}{r}=\frac{(6561.72)(0)+(0)(5.5387)+(0)(5.5387)}{6561.72}=0\text{ km/s} \\
		\dot{\phi}&=\frac{x\dot{y}-y\dot{x}}{x^2+y^2}=\frac{(6561.72)(5.5387)-(0)(0)}{(6561.72)^2+(0)^2}\left(\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}\right)\approx0.048363\text{ deg/s} \\
		\rho&=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}=\sqrt{(6561.72)^2+(0)^2}=6561.72\text{ km} \\
		\dot{\theta}&=\frac{\rho^2\dot{z}-z\left(x\dot{x}+y\dot{y}\right)}{\rho r^2} \\
		&=\frac{(6561.72)^2(5.5387)-(0)\left((6561.72)(0)+(0)(5.5387)\right)}{(6561.72)(6561.72)^2}\left(\frac{180^\circ}{\pi}\right)\approx0.048363\text{ deg/s}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

\end{equation}

Then, the EoM in Eq.~(\ref{eq:eom}) must be put in a form compatible with the numerical integrator. This works simply if we integrate an orbital spherical coordinate state of $\boldsymbol{x}=[r,\phi,\theta,\dot{r},\dot{\phi},\dot{\theta}]^T$, whose time derivative is:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\dot{\boldsymbol{x}}&=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\dot{r} \\
			\dot{\phi} \\
			\dot{\theta} \\
			\ddot{r} \\
			\ddot{\phi} \\
			\ddot{\theta} \\
		\end{bmatrix}
		=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\dot{r} \\
			\dot{\phi} \\
			\dot{\theta} \\
			-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{r}}{2m}+r\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+r\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^2}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^4}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right) \\
			-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}}{r}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\tan\theta \\
			-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r}-\cos\theta\sin\theta\left(\dot{\phi}^2+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^5}\right) \\
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		&\text{Where:  }v=\sqrt{\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2}
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:stateomdrag}
\end{equation}

			-\frac{(0)A\rho(r)v\dot{\phi}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}}{r}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\tan\theta \\
			-\frac{(0)A\rho(r)v\dot{\theta}}{2m}-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\theta}}{r}-\cos\theta\sin\theta\left(\dot{\phi}^2+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{r^5}\right) \\
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		&=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\dot{r} \\
			\dot{\phi} \\
			\dot{\theta} \\
			r\dot{\phi}^2\cos^2\theta+r\dot{\theta}^2-\frac{\mu}{r^2}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^4}\left(3\sin^2\theta-1\right) \\
			-\frac{2\dot{r}\dot{\phi}}{r}+2\dot{\phi}\dot{\theta}\tan\theta \\

\subsubsection*{(c.3): Find}

For both models, plot the normalized changes in $H_z$, $\mathcal{J}$:
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\mathcal{J}(t)-\mathcal{J}(0)}{|\mathcal{J}(0)|},\quad\frac{H_z(t)-H_z(0)}{|H_z(0)|}
	\label{eq:normalizedchange}
\end{equation}
Discuss why the $J_2$-only result makes sense using the symmetry of the potential. For the drag result, compare the signs and wualitative behavior against $dH_z/dt$ and $d\mathcal{J}/dt$ derived above.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

From part (b.5) in Eq.~(\ref{eq:jacobiintegral}), the Jacobi integral for both models was found:
\begin{equation}
	\mathcal{J}=\frac{m\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{mr^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}-\frac{m\mu}{r}+\frac{3m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta-\frac{m\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}
\end{equation}

And in part (b.4) in Eq.~(\ref{eq:zmomentum}), the function for $H_z$ for both models was found:
\begin{equation}
	H_z=mr^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta
\end{equation}

Since the spacecraft mass $m$ is not specified, we instead evaluate both quantities per unit mass:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\frac{\mathcal{J}}{m}&=\frac{\dot{r}^2}{2}+\frac{r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2}{2}+\frac{r^2\dot{\theta}^2}{2}-\frac{\mu}{r}+\frac{3\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3}\sin^2\theta-\frac{\mu J_2r_o^2}{2r^3} \\
		\\
		\frac{H_z}{m}&=r^2\dot{\phi}\cos^2\theta
	\end{split}
	\label{eq:massnorm}
\end{equation}

Utilizing the initial conditions found in (c.1) Eq.~(\ref{eq:initialcons}) and the values defined in table (\ref{tab:reference_orbit}), the values of $\mathcal{J}(t)$ and $H_z(t)$ are computed over the simulation time of both models using the expressions above, starting from their initial values $\mathcal{J}(0)$ and $H_z(0)$. As requested by the problem, the differences $\mathcal{J}(t)-\mathcal{J}(0)$ and $H_z(t)-H_z(0)$ are plotted over time, normalized by the absolute value of their initial values.

\begin{mdframed}[linewidth=1pt, innertopmargin=10pt, innerbottommargin=10pt]
	The normalized changes for the $J_2$ only model are:
	\begin{figure}[H]
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=0.85\textwidth]{c3_J2only_normalized.png}
		\caption{Normalized changes in $\mathcal{J}$ and $H_z$ for the $J_2$ only model over 5 days.}
		\label{fig:c3_J2only_normalized}
	\end{figure}
	We see in the results above, that the values of $\mathcal{J}$ and $H_z$ (when normalized to their initial values) do not vary further than the range of $10^{-12}$ from their initial values, which is in the range of numeric drift. This can be interpreted to mean that in the no-drag model, $\mathcal{J}$ and $H_z$ do not change over time.\\
	
	The $J_2$-only result makes sense here because of the symmetries of the potential.\\
	
	First, for the angular momentum about $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$ $H_z$, the $J_2$ potential seen in Eq.~(\ref{eq:gravpotential}) depends only on $r$ and $\theta$, so it is symmetric about the $\hat{\boldsymbol{e}}_z$ axis. This means $\phi$ is a cyclic coordinate, $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\phi}=0$, and with no drag the Lagrange equation for $\phi$ reduces to $\frac{d}{dt}\left(\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}\right)=0$. As established in (b.4), the generalized momentum $\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{\phi}}$ is equal to $H_z$, so $H_z$ cannot change over time in this model.\\
	
	Second, for the Jacobi integral, the $J_2$ potential Eq.~(\ref{eq:gravpotential}) is also symmetric in time, since it has no explicit dependence on $t$, so $\frac{\partial L}{\partial t}=0$. With no non-conservative forces, Eq.~(\ref{eq:jacobirate}) then becomes $\frac{d\mathcal{J}}{dt}=0$, so $\mathcal{J}$ cannot change over time either. Both results are consistent with the plots above, where the only variation is numerical drift.
\end{mdframed}

	Recall from part (b.6) the equations for $dH_z/dt$ and $d\mathcal{J}/dt$ were derived to be:
	\begin{equation}
		\frac{d}{dt}H_z=-\frac{C_DA\rho(r)v}{2m}H_z,\quad\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^3
	\end{equation}
	
	In the analysis in part (b.6), it was identified that the quantities used in these expressions would never cause the sign to flip, meaning we should expect to see $H_z$ and $\mathcal{J}$ to continuously decrease due to the negative sign in both time derivative expressions. This is consistent with the figure above which shows the two quantities continuously deviating in the negative direction from their initial values with a smooth curve and at an order of magnitude that would rule out numeric drift.\\
	
	We also see both curves steepen over time, which is consistent with the time derivative expressions. If angular momentum and generalized total energy (aka Jacobi integral) are decreasing, then we should expect the orbit to decay, lowering the average altitude of the spacecraft along its orbit. Since the rates of $H_z$ and $\mathcal{J}$ both scale with $\rho(r)$, which grows exponentially as altitude decreases, and with $v$, which increases as the orbit decays, the decay of the orbit quickens the rate at which $H_z$ and $\mathcal{J}$ decrease. For $H_z$, this growth in $\rho(r)$ appears to outpace the slowing effect of its rate being proportional to $H_z$ itself.
\end{mdframed}

\subsubsection*{(c.4): Find}

Augment the state vector by the work state ($\boldsymbol{Q}_D$: drag-derived generalized force)
\begin{equation}
	\dot{W}_D=\boldsymbol{Q}_D^T\dot{\boldsymbol{q}},\quad W_D(0)=0
\end{equation}
and propagate the orbit under drag again. Plot the normalized work-balance residual
\begin{equation}
	\frac{\mathcal{J}(t)-\mathcal{J}(0)-W_D(t)}{|\mathcal{J}(0)|}
	\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
Discuss whether it is consistent with your integrator tolerance.\\

\textbf{Analysis/Conclusions:}\\

Recall from part (b.2) the generalized drag force $\boldsymbol{Q}_D$ was found to be:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		Q_r&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,\dot{r} \\
		Q_\phi&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi} \\
		Q_\theta&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta}
	\end{split}
\end{equation}

When performing the dot product with the generalized coordinate derivative $\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}$ to get the work state derivative $\dot{W}_D$, we get:
\begin{equation}
	\begin{split}
		\dot{W}_D&=\boldsymbol{Q}_D^T\dot{\boldsymbol{q}}=
		\begin{bmatrix}
			Q_r & Q_\phi & Q_\theta \\
		\end{bmatrix}
		\begin{bmatrix}
			\dot{r} \\
			\dot{\phi} \\
			\dot{\theta} \\
		\end{bmatrix} \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,\dot{r}^2-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\,r^2\dot{\theta}^2 \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v\left(\dot{r}^2+r^2\cos^2\theta\,\dot{\phi}^2+r^2\dot{\theta}^2\right) \\
		&=-\frac{1}{2}C_DA\rho(r)v^3
	\end{split}
\end{equation}
Which is the same expression derived for $\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{J}$, which means the residual in Eq.~(\ref{eq:residual}) should stay near zero within the expected range from the integration tolerance. Note that since $A$ and $m$ are not individually specified, only their ratio $A/m$, the work state is evaluated per unit mass, the same as $\mathcal{J}$ and $H_z$ were in (c.3).\\
